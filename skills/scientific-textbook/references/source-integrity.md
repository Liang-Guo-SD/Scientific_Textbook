# Source and Claim Integrity

Use this reference when textbook claims depend on literature, standards, current facts, external datasets, or reported empirical results.

## Source policy

Define acceptable source classes before large-scale drafting. Prefer primary research, authoritative standards, official datasets, and recognized reference works when they directly support the claim. Use secondary sources for orientation or synthesis and label their role.

For each material claim, record enough information to recover the source and verify scope, normally author or authority, title, publication, date or edition, stable identifier or URL, access date when relevant, and the exact claim supported.

## Verification rules

- Open and inspect the source before citing it; do not cite search snippets or remembered references as evidence.
- Verify that the source supports the wording, population, conditions, units, and causal strength of the claim.
- Check revision, effective date, retraction, correction, or supersession when material.
- Preserve uncertainty, disagreement, null results, and missing evidence.
- Distinguish a source reporting a result from independent replication of that result.
- Never invent bibliographic details, quotations, page numbers, dataset properties, standards, or experimental outcomes.
- Keep quotations short and compliant; prefer accurate paraphrase tied to the source.

## Claim classes

Classify material statements as established definition or result, sourced empirical observation, interpretation, modeling choice, recommendation, or research hypothesis. Match language strength to evidence strength.

## High-stakes boundary

For medical, clinical, safety-critical, legal, regulated, or operational guidance, require current authoritative sources and qualified human review. State jurisdiction, population, intended educational use, and non-applicability boundaries. The skill may organize evidence but must not claim certification or replace professional judgment.

## Review evidence

At review, sample claims from every chapter and trace them to sources. Increase sampling or require full traceability when errors could cause harm, when sources are unstable, or when the book makes novel syntheses. Record unresolved conflicts as limitations or open findings rather than smoothing them into consensus.
